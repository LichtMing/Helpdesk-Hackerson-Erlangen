What is a CT scan? A CT (computed tomography) scan uses X-rays and a computer to create detailed cross-sectional images of the inside of your body. It helps doctors see organs, bones and blood vessels very clearly. The scanner is a large ring, and you lie on a table that moves slowly through it.

How long does the CT scan take? The scan itself usually takes only a few minutes. Including preparation, changing and any waiting time, you should plan for about 30 minutes to one hour at the department. If you need to drink a contrast liquid beforehand, it can take longer.

Do I need to fast before the CT scan? For many CT scans you can eat and drink normally. If your scan uses contrast agent, you may be asked not to eat for a few hours beforehand, but you can usually drink water. Your appointment letter or the staff will tell you the exact rule for your scan.

What should I wear? Wear comfortable clothes without metal, such as zips, buttons or underwire. You may be asked to change into a hospital gown. Please remove jewelry, glasses, hairpins, hearing aids and removable dental work from the area being scanned, because metal can disturb the images.

What is contrast agent? Contrast agent is a liquid that makes blood vessels and organs show up more clearly on the images. It is usually given through a small needle (cannula) in a vein in your arm. Some scans use a contrast drink instead.

What does contrast agent feel like? When the contrast agent is injected, many people feel a warm sensation spreading through the body, a metallic taste in the mouth, or a feeling as if they need to pass urine. These feelings are normal and harmless, and they pass within a minute or two.

What if I have allergies or kidney problems? Please tell the staff before your scan if you have ever had a reaction to contrast agent, if you have allergies, kidney problems, diabetes, or thyroid problems. The staff will check whether any special precautions are needed. The robot cannot assess this for you.

What if I am pregnant or breastfeeding? Please tell the staff before the scan if you are or might be pregnant, or if you are breastfeeding. The doctor will decide together with you whether and how the scan should be done.

What happens during the CT scan? You lie on your back on the scanner table, usually with your arms above your head. The table moves slowly through the ring while the images are taken. The technologist leaves the room during the scan but can see you through a window and talk to you through a speaker at all times.

Do I have to hold my breath? For some scans, you will be asked to hold your breath for a few seconds so the images are sharp. The machine or the technologist will tell you exactly when to breathe in, hold, and breathe out. It is important to lie as still as possible during the scan.

Is the CT scan loud? The scanner makes a soft whirring or humming noise while it rotates. It is much quieter than an MRI scan. You do not need ear protection.

I am afraid of tight spaces. Is the CT scanner narrow? The CT scanner is an open ring, not a long tunnel. Your head is usually outside the ring or only briefly inside it, and the scan is quick. If you feel anxious, please tell the staff. They can explain each step and stay in contact with you throughout.

Does the CT scan hurt? The scan itself is painless. If you receive contrast agent, you will feel a small prick when the cannula is placed in your arm, similar to a blood test.

How much radiation does a CT scan use? A CT scan uses X-rays, which is a form of radiation. Modern scanners use the lowest dose needed for good images. Your doctor has requested the scan because the benefit of the information is greater than the small risk from the radiation.

Can someone come with me into the scan room? A companion can usually wait in the waiting area. During the scan itself, other people normally cannot stay in the room because of the X-rays. In special cases, for example with children, a parent may be allowed to stay with protective clothing. Please ask the staff.

What happens after the CT scan? Most people can go home and return to normal activities right away, including driving. If you received contrast agent, the cannula is removed after the scan, and you may be asked to wait a short time to make sure you feel well. Please drink plenty of water during the rest of the day to help your body flush out the contrast agent.

When will I get my results? A specialist doctor called a radiologist will look at your images and write a report. The report is sent to the doctor who referred you, who will discuss the results with you. The robot and the technologists cannot give you your results.

What should I do if I feel unwell? If you feel unwell, dizzy, itchy, short of breath or have any pain at any time before, during or after the scan, tell the staff immediately. The robot will call a staff member for you.
